Plant Families

Family Names

Family names of plants almost always finish with -aceae. The first part is typically a genus that is well known in the family. Dipterocarpaceae is named after Dipterocarpus and Lauraceae is named after Laurus, the bay laurel you use in your cooking.

About This Page

A family of plants is a collection of plants that share characteristics and traits that are important such as the structure of flowers and fruit. On this page, you'll be introduced to the two families we are highlighting on Pl@netMatrix. Click on the links in each section to view their genera and species.

On this page

  • Dipterocarpaceae

How to Tell the Two Families Apart

  1. Look at the fruit. The fruits of dipterocarps are winged, and those of laurels are fleshy berries.
  2. Break open a leaf. In most cases, laurel leaves have a spicy or citrus fragrance.
  3. Look for sticky sweet smelling resin in the bark, this is a good indication of a dipterocarp.
  4. Look closely at the leaves. Many laurels have glossy, waxy leaves with a pointed “drip tip”.

Dipterocarpaceae

The dipterocarp family

The Dipterocarpaceae are a family of flowering plants, consisting of approximately 22 genera and nearly 700 species, the majority of which are trees of the lowland tropical forest. They are found throughout the tropics, from northern South America and Africa to India, Southeast Asia and the Philippines. But Borneo hosts the most diversity and lowland rainforest in Borneo has been sustained for millions of years by dipterocarps. Many are giants, towering over the other trees 40 to 70 metres tall. A yellow meranti in Danum Valley, Sabah is the tallest known. It is an incredible 93 metres tall and is known as Menara which means “tower” in Malay.

The name is derived from the Greek: "fruit with two wings". Sadly, almost all the species of Borneo are on the verge of extinction. Dipterocarps provide the majority of the region's wood, plywood, resin and fragrant oils; and too many are being logged or felled illegally and even being removed with the forest.

Source: Wikipedia

Quick facts

Scientific name
Dipterocarpaceae
Size
22 genera, about 695 species
Largest genera
Shorea (196 species), Hopea (104), Dipterocarpus (70) and Vatica (65)
Origin and range
Throughout the tropics, especially in Borneo
Subfamilies
Dipterocarpoideae (mainly Asia) and Monotoideae (Africa, Madagascar and the Colombian Amazon)
Genera on this site
Dipterocarpus and Monotes
Species on this site
D. alatus, D. tuberculatus, M. adenophyllus and M. glaber

How to recognise it

  • Fruits with papery wings: two in Dipterocarpus, more in some other genera
  • Tall, straight trunks, usually rising over the rest of the forest
  • Fragrant, resinous wood
  • Can be evergreen or lose their leaves, and grow anywhere from sea level up to about 1,300 metres

Lauraceae

The laurel family

The laurel family, Lauraceae, is made up of the true bay laurel and its close relatives. It's a large family, with 2,850-3,500 species divided into over 50 genera. They grow in warm and tropical places all over the world, especially Southeast Asia and South America. Most of them are evergreen, fragrant trees and shrubs. Some, such as Sassafras, shed foliage annually; and one genus, Cassytha, consists entirely of vines that live off other plants. Laurels are a significant component of the forests from lowland to mountain areas, and give their name to the shiny-leaved 'laurel forests' and the cloud forests on either side of the equator. The family is also of ancient origin: fossil flowers have been found dating back to the middle of the Cretaceous period (between 90 and 98 million years ago).

A large number of laurels contain natural oils that deter animals from ingesting them. There have been many uses found for them; cinnamon and cassia, camphor, bay leaves and avocados are all members of this family and laurel wood is used for timber and for chests to keep insects away. The fruit is a single seeded berry and can be borne in an acorn like cup. It is a favorite food of birds, monkeys and other animals, who also carry the seeds about. Unfortunately, many species are in danger of extinction due to their overcutting and illegal logging and their loss of forest homes.

Source: Wikipedia

Quick facts

Scientific name
Lauraceae
Size
58 accepted genera and an estimated 2,850 to 3,500 species (estimates vary)
Origin and range
Warm temperate and tropical regions throughout the world, particularly Southeast Asia and South America
Popular members of the family
Cinnamon and cassia (Cinnamomum), camphor (Camphora), bay laurel (Laurus) and avocado (Persea)
Subfamilies
Lauroideae and Cassythoideae (Cassytha only)
Age
Fossils from the mid-Cretaceous, 90 to 98 million years old
Genera on this site
Beilschmiedia and Cryptocarya
Species on this site
B. berteroana, B. costaricensis, C. adpressa and C. alba

How to recognise it

  • Fragrant leaves, bark and wood, naturally full of oils
  • Pointed oval leaves, many of which are shiny and waxy, with a "drip tip" that allows rain to drain away.
  • One-seeded fruits that can be borne in a small cup, like an acorn
  • Flowers are female first and male later (protogynous) to reduce inbreeding.
