Plant Genera

Named After People

Many genus names honour scientists. Beilschmiedia is named after the German botanist Carl Traugott Beilschmied. Other names describe the plant instead: Dipterocarpus means "two-winged fruit" and Cryptocarya means "hidden nut".

About This Page

A genus (plural: genera) is a smaller group of closely related species inside a family. The genus name is always written first in a scientific name, in italics and with a capital letter. Here are two genera from each of our featured families.

On this page

  • Dipterocarpus
  • Monotes

  • Cryptocarya

Four Questions to Identify the Genus

  1. Which family does the plant belong to?
  2. Does the fruit have wings? If so, count them: two long wings suggests Dipterocarpus, five equal wings suggests Monotes.
  3. If the fruit is fleshy, is the seed completely hidden inside? That suggests Cryptocarya.
  4. Is the fruit olive-shaped with no cup at its base? That suggests Beilschmiedia.

Dipterocarpus

Keruing

Dipterocarpus is the genus that gave the whole family its name. Its trees are known in Malaysia as keruing, and they are some of the most important timber trees in the region. Most are large canopy trees with thick, rough bark and big, stiff leaves.

The trunk can be tapped for an oily resin known as keruing oil, which has been used for centuries to waterproof boats and make torches. The fruit has two long wings and three short ones, which slow its fall and help the wind carry it away from the parent tree.

Quick facts

Family
Dipterocarpaceae
Common name
Keruing
Size
About 70 species
Origin and range
South and Southeast Asia, from India and Sri Lanka to the Philippines; richest in Borneo
Species on this site
Dipterocarpus alatus and Dipterocarpus tuberculatus

How to recognise it

  • Large leaves with straight, evenly spaced side veins
  • Two long wings and three short wings on each fruit
  • Big leaf buds protected by a folded scale that leaves a ring scar on the twig

Monotes

African dipterocarps

Monotes shows how varied the dipterocarp family can be. Instead of giant rainforest trees, these are small trees and shrubs of the dry, open woodlands of Africa known as miombo. They survive long dry seasons and regular grass fires, often dropping their leaves when water is scarce.

Their fruits have five equal wings rather than two long ones, and their leaves are usually broad and rounded. Many species also have a small gland near the base of the leaf, which botanists use to tell them apart.

Quick facts

Family
Dipterocarpaceae
Common name
Often simply called monotes
Size
About 30 species
Origin and range
Tropical Africa and Madagascar
Species on this site
Monotes adenophyllus and Monotes glaber

How to recognise it

  • Small, often crooked trees of dry woodland
  • Broad, rounded leaves with a net of fine veins
  • Fruits with five equal wings

Beilschmiedia

Wild laurels

Beilschmiedia is one of the largest genera in the laurel family and is found on almost every warm continent, including Borneo. Most species are evergreen trees with smooth, glossy leaves that grow in the middle and upper layers of moist forests.

Their fruits are fleshy and shaped like olives or small plums. Unlike many other laurels, the fruit does not sit in a cup. Birds and mammals eat the fruit and spread the large seeds, so these trees depend on healthy wildlife to regenerate.

Quick facts

Family
Lauraceae
Named after
Carl Traugott Beilschmied, a German botanist
Size
About 250 species
Origin and range
Tropical and warm regions of Asia, Africa, Australia, New Zealand and the Americas
Species on this site
Beilschmiedia berteroana and Beilschmiedia costaricensis

How to recognise it

  • Glossy, smooth-edged evergreen leaves, often growing in pairs
  • Tiny greenish flowers in branched clusters
  • Olive-shaped fruits without a cup at the base

Cryptocarya

Hidden-nut laurels

The name Cryptocarya means "hidden nut". After flowering, the base of the flower grows up and completely wraps around the developing seed, so the nut inside is hidden from view. This is the feature that sets the genus apart from other laurels.

Many species have leaves that are dark and glossy on top but pale blue-green or whitish underneath. They range from rainforest trees in Southeast Asia and Australia to hardy trees of the dry scrub of central Chile.

Quick facts

Family
Lauraceae
Meaning of the name
Greek for "hidden nut"
Size
About 350 species
Origin and range
Tropical and subtropical Asia, Australia, Africa, Madagascar and South America
Species on this site
Cryptocarya adpressa and Cryptocarya alba

How to recognise it

  • Seed completely enclosed by the fleshy fruit wall
  • Leaves often pale or bluish underneath
  • Round or olive-shaped fruits that ripen green, red or black
