Plant Explorer

Why Scientific Names?

Common names change from place to place. One tree can have a Malay name, an English name and a local Iban name. A scientific name is the same everywhere, so scientists around the world know they are talking about exactly the same plant.

Scientific names are written in italics, with the genus first and the species second, for example Vatica borneensis.

Discover Malaysia's Forest Plants

The Explorer works like a small plant encyclopedia. Scientists sort every living plant into groups, from very broad to very specific. On this site we focus on three of those levels: families, genera and species. Each level is linked to the others, so you can start with a family and move down to its genera and species, or start with a species and work your way back up.

We have chosen two plant families that are important in Malaysian forests. The Dipterocarpaceae family includes many of the giant trees that form the rainforest canopy and supply valuable timber. The Myristicaceae family, also called the nutmeg family, contains aromatic trees whose seeds are covered in a bright red layer. Several members of both families are now considered endangered, rare or threatened.

How to Use the Explorer

  1. Choose a classification level from the cards above or the Explorer drop-down menu.
  2. Read about the group, its photos and where it is found.
  3. Follow the links to move up or down to related families, genera or species.
  4. Found the plant in the wild? Confirm it on the Identify page.

Key Terms

Family
A large group of related genera that share important features, such as the shape of their flowers or fruit.
Genus (plural: genera)
A group of closely related species. The genus name is always written first in a scientific name.
Species
A single kind of plant whose members can breed with each other. It is the most specific level used here.
ERT
Short for Endangered, Rare and Threatened, the plants this project most wants to monitor.
