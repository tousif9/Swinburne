Plant Families

Family Names

Plant family names almost always end in -aceae. The first part comes from one important genus in the family: Dipterocarpaceae is named after Dipterocarpus, and Lauraceae after Laurus, the bay laurel used in cooking.

About This Page

A plant family is a large group of related plants that share key features, such as the structure of their flowers and fruit. This page introduces the two families featured on Pl@netMatrix. Follow the links in each section to explore their genera and species.

On this page

  • Dipterocarpaceae
  • Lauraceae

How to Tell the Two Families Apart

  1. Look at the fruit: dipterocarps have winged fruits, laurels have fleshy berries.
  2. Crush a leaf gently: laurel leaves usually smell spicy or citrusy.
  3. Check the bark for sticky, fragrant resin, which is typical of dipterocarps.
  4. Look at the flowers: laurel flowers are tiny, with parts in groups of three.

Dipterocarpaceae

The dipterocarp family

The Dipterocarpaceae are the giants of the Southeast Asian rainforest. In the lowland forests of Malaysia they often make up most of the tallest trees, and some species rise above the canopy to more than 60 metres. The name comes from Greek words meaning "two-winged fruit", because many species produce seeds with long wings that spin like helicopters as they fall.

These trees are very valuable for timber, sold under trade names such as meranti and keruing, and many also produce a sticky resin once used for varnish, torches and boat repairs. Heavy logging and forest clearing mean that many dipterocarps are now listed as endangered, rare or threatened.

Quick facts

Common name
Dipterocarp family
Order
Malvales
Size
Around 16 genera and several hundred species
Origin and range
Tropical Asia, Africa and South America, with the greatest variety in Borneo
Genera on this site
Dipterocarpus and Monotes
Species on this site
D. alatus, D. tuberculatus, M. adenophyllus, M. glaber

How to recognise it

  • Tall, straight trunks with a crown high in or above the canopy
  • Fragrant resin that oozes from cuts in the bark
  • Fruits with wings formed from enlarged sepals (the outer parts of the flower)
  • Many species flower together only every few years in a "mass flowering" event

Lauraceae

The laurel family

The Lauraceae, or laurel family, are aromatic trees and shrubs. Their leaves and bark contain tiny oil cells, so they often smell spicy or citrusy when crushed. Several familiar plants belong to this family, including cinnamon, bay leaf, camphor and avocado.

In Malaysian rainforests, laurels form a large part of the middle and upper layers of the forest. Their fruits are rich, oily berries that feed hornbills, pigeons and many other animals, which carry the seeds far from the parent tree. Because many species look very alike, laurels are known as one of the hardest plant families to identify.

Quick facts

Common name
Laurel family
Order
Laurales
Size
About 50 genera and between 2,500 and 3,000 species
Origin and range
Tropical and warm regions worldwide, especially Southeast Asia and tropical America
Genera on this site
Beilschmiedia and Cryptocarya
Species on this site
B. berteroana, B. costaricensis, C. adpressa, C. alba

How to recognise it

  • Glossy, leathery leaves that are aromatic when crushed
  • Small green, yellow or cream flowers with parts in groups of three
  • Berry-like fruits, often sitting in a small cup
  • Many species have colourful red or bronze new leaves
